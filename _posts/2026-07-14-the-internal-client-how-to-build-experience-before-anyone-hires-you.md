"You need experience to get experience."

If you have ever job hunted, you have run into this wall. Every entry level listing wants two to three years of experience. Every internship wants a portfolio. Every portfolio needs real projects. And every real project needs someone to trust you with their work first.

It is a closed loop. You are ready to work, capable of doing the work, but locked out of the work because nobody will hand you the first chance.

## The Experience Trap

This is the Experience Trap, and it hits hardest if you are a self-directed learner or a career changer, especially in a developing economy where formal apprenticeships, structured internships, and warm professional networks are not always available to you. You cannot always intern your way in. You cannot always know someone who knows someone. So what do you do while you wait for permission that may never come?

Stop waiting. And stop only applying.

## What To Do Instead

Do not sit back and hope the right job finds you. Go out and gather the exact experience that will eventually make you findable.

The jobs that reach you this way will not be ones you submit a CV for. You get contacted because there is evidence of work you have already done. Call it "man know man" if you like, but it is the evidence kind. People do not vouch for you because they like you. They vouch for you because they can see what you have built.

That shift, from chasing permission to creating proof, is the whole idea behind the Internal Client Method.

## The Internal Client Method, in Short

The method is simple to state and takes real discipline to practice: treat yourself as your own client.

Instead of waiting for a company to give you a brief, write your own. Instead of waiting for a manager to assign you a project, assign it to yourself, and complete it to the same standard you would if a paying client were watching. Produce real, usable, evaluable work. Then let that work speak for you.

This is not practice for practice's sake. Practice projects stay in a drawer. Internal Client work is built to be shown. It becomes your evidence, your portfolio, your case for why someone should trust you with the real thing.

## Why This Matters More Than a Resume

A resume tells someone what you claim you can do. Evidence shows them. In a market that is skeptical of claims and short on time to verify them, evidence moves faster than a CV ever will.

This is why the people who reach out to you this way are not reacting to an application. They are reacting to proof they can see and evaluate for themselves. That is a different kind of trust, and it opens different kinds of doors.
